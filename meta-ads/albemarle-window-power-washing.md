# Meta Ads — TideWater Window Cleaning (Albemarle County)

**4 bodies × 10 hooks × 3 CTAs = 40 to 120 ads from one filming day.**

Built on the GOATed Ads assembly method in this repo (`$100M Goated ads`, `$100M Hooks`, `$100M Proof Checklist`): 80% of the effort goes into hooks and 20% into the body. You film the body once and stitch a new hook onto the front of it.

---

## 0. How Meta works now and what it means for these ads

- **Your creative does the targeting now.** Meta's delivery system (Andromeda) mostly ignores the narrow interest targeting you set. It reads each ad and finds the people that ad fits. So the **hook is your targeting**. A hook about "well water spots in Earlysville" finds rural homeowners on well water. A hook about "Hosting Thanksgiving?" finds people about to host. Target broad and let the hooks split up the audience.
- **Change the first frame, not just the words.** Meta groups ads that look almost the same and treats them as one ad. If all 10 hooks are you saying different lines in the same spot, Meta may treat them as one ad. Every hook below comes with its **own opening shot** (a different place, person, format or prop). Film it that way.
- **Meta's personal-attributes policy.** You can't assert or imply things like a viewer's age, health, disability or financial situation ("Are you a retiree?", "Got a bad back?", "Can't afford…"). The hooks below talk around that ("If climbing a ladder isn't your idea of a Saturday…"). Calling out a location ("Crozet homeowners") is fine.
- **Proof wins.** Hormozi's audit of his own ad account found that about 80% of his winning ads "told one story: this product works." That's why your process body worked: it *showed* the work. The reviews body should do the same thing for trust.
- **Hopkins' rules (*Scientific Advertising*, in this repo). Every line in this doc has to pass them:**
  1. **Never insult the customer.** Blame the pollen, the clay, the well water or the paper towels, never the homeowner's effort, home or judgment. Never imply neglect. Hopkins: *don't say "Why do you neglect this offer?" That suggests that people are neglecting.*
  2. **Show the bright side.** Lead with the result and the feeling (sun through spotless glass, the view, a guest-ready home), not the grime. Hopkins found positive ads outpulled negative ones *"four to one."* Problem footage only appears as a quick moment inside a demonstration of your work.
  3. **Never knock competitors.** No "most window cleaners skip…" and no "the last guy ghosted you." *"To attack a rival is never good advertising."*
  4. **Tell people what to do, not what to avoid.** "3 questions to ask," not "Don't hire anyone until…"
  5. **Be specific.** "Almost 50 reviews," "30 days," "8 spots," "screens hand-washed." *"Platitudes and generalities roll off the human understanding."*
  6. **Offer service, don't beg for the sale.** The free quote is your "sample." Assume they'll act ("Tap below to grab a spot"), and invite them to follow the crowd ("your neighbors in Old Trail").
  7. **Give the reason why** (the slow-season discount) and **test everything** (the Body-Hook-CTA naming is your keyed test).
- **Your own data says the same thing: your best ads show someone cleaning.** Either Gabe talking while he cleans, or the crew cleaning on screen. So that's the rule for every ad in this doc:
  1. **Every body is filmed while someone is cleaning.** No sitting-down talking heads.
  2. **Cleaning is on screen by second 3 of every ad.** If a hook opens on something else (a map, a phone, an AI problem shot), cut to real cleaning within 2–3 seconds.

## 1. Production plan

### ✅ Decision (Gabe): the on-camera talent is an AI version of Gabe, voiced by Gabe's real recordings

- **Voice = always Gabe's real audio.** Record each body (loose, unscripted, real "um"s) and each hook line on your phone. That audio drives the AI lip-sync. Never use an AI-generated voice; that's what makes it sound fake.
- **Face = an AI character built from Gabe's real photos** (`@gabe` character sheet; partners get `@partner1` and `@partner2` for Body D).
- **Cleaning = real B-roll wherever possible.** AI-Gabe is shown on the job site *pausing* to talk (squeegee in hand, ladder behind him), and the edit cuts to real B-roll for the actual cleaning. AI still gets hands, squeegee strokes and water wrong, so keep AI-Gabe's cleaning motion minimal.
- **Testimonials (Body E) stay 100% real customers.** AI testimonials are banned by the FTC.
- **Expect Meta's "AI info" label** on some ads. Test AI-Gabe ads against your existing real winners and let the numbers decide.

### Hooks are scripted. Bodies sound unscripted.

- **Hook:** exact words, short and punchy. Said word for word or put on screen with a voiceover. This is the part you polish.
- **Body:** has to sound like you're making it up on the spot, with pauses, "um"s, restarts and side comments. People scroll past anything that sounds like an ad. They stop for someone who's just talking.

**How to get a body that sounds natural:**

1. **Don't memorize the example takes.** Each body below has **talking points** (the beats you have to hit) and an **example take** that shows how it should *sound*. Read the example once to get the feel, then put it away and say it your way. A memorized "um" sounds more fake than a script.
2. **Always be cleaning on camera.** This is what wins for you. Gabe talks *while* squeegeeing, pulling screens or running the surface cleaner, or talks while the crew works in the frame behind him. Prop the phone on a ladder or have someone hold it. When your hands are busy, you also talk more naturally. Pause the work for a second when you want to make a point, then go back to it.
3. **Talk to one person.** Picture a neighbor leaning over the fence asking "so what do you guys actually do?" and answer them.
4. **Do 3–5 takes and keep the most real one** that still hits every talking point. It's usually not the cleanest take.
5. **Keep the stumbles in editing.** Only cut dead air longer than about a second, or a tangent that loses the thread. Leave the "um"s, restarts, laughs and "oh, look at this."
6. **Phone camera, natural light, no teleprompter.** A polished look makes it read as an ad again.

### What can be AI and what has to be real

**Rule: AI can set the *scene* and show the *bright side* (sun through clear glass, a guest-ready front door). It can't show *your* crew's work, *your* customers or *your* reviews.**

**The bodies have to be real people and real footage.** Three reasons:
1. **AI can't fake "unscripted" yet.** AI voices and avatars saying "um" and pausing sound uncanny. The natural feel you want only comes from you actually talking.
2. **The body is the proof.** It's the part that says "this company is real and does good work." If it's AI, there's no proof in it.
3. **Real cleaning is what makes your winners win.** It's also what AI video gets wrong most visibly: squeegee strokes, water sheeting off glass, a surface cleaner on concrete. If people are stopping to watch real work, that's the one part you never fake.

**Legal lines:**
- **No AI-generated customers, testimonials or reviews.** The FTC rule on fake reviews and testimonials (in effect since October 2024) bans them, and that includes AI-generated ones.
- **No AI before/afters passed off as your work.** That's deceptive advertising. Locals will also notice when your real job doesn't match the ad.
- Meta may add an **"AI info"** label to ads it detects as AI-made. That's fine on a hook shot. It's another reason to keep the body real.

**Making AI hook clips:**
- **Make them look like Albemarle:** brick colonials, farmhouses, vinyl siding, Blue Ridge in the background, Virginia trees. No palm trees or stucco.
- **Use your reference photos.** Upload photos of the homes and areas you work in, and the AI problem shots can match your actual service area. If your equipment shows up in an AI shot (e.g., your ladder against a house), use the equipment photos so it matches your real gear. That way the cut from the AI hook to the real body doesn't jump.
- **AI makes the "different first frame" rule cheap** (see section 0). Generate a different setting for each hook so Meta treats them as separate ads.
- **Keep each clip 2–4 seconds,** then cut to real footage when the body starts.

### Your existing B-roll comes first

You already have real cleaning B-roll (crew working, no talking). **Use it before AI, every time.**

- **Bodies = Gabe's real audio over the real B-roll.** Record yourself talking through each body's talking points (loose, unscripted), and lay it over the cleaning footage. That's real voice plus real cleaning, the same ingredients as your winners. On-camera clips of Gabe cleaning are still the best version when you can get them.
- **Body B:** audio plus review screenshots over the B-roll. **Body D:** all three owners' audio over the B-roll, with real photos of the three of you cut in when each one starts talking.
- **Hooks your B-roll already covers:** A-H01 (squeegee stripe), A-H07 (surface cleaner on concrete), C-H01 (text over a squeegee shot).

- **Check your footage first:** A-H02 (a clear window with a mountain view). Use AI only if you don't have it.
- **Still need AI or graphics:** A-H05 (sun through a spotless window), B-H08 (yard sign at a sparkling house), C-H02 (sunlit holiday door), C-H04 (raindrops, then sun), C-H05 (ladder hung up in the garage), C-H08 (whole house sparkling in the sun), plus graphics for B-H09, C-H06 and D-H06 (friendly text mockup).
- **Hooks with Gabe on camera** (B-H03, C-H07, C-H10, the D hooks, etc.) need a quick phone clip. If you can't film one, turn it into a voiceover over B-roll; it'll be a bit weaker.

### Filming day (real footage only)

1. **Bodies on one real job (2–3 hours).** Film B-roll throughout: truck arriving, shoes coming off at the door, pulling screens, scrubbing, squeegee passes, track vacuuming, the walk-through, surface cleaner on concrete, soft wash on siding, before/after splits. You'll reuse this footage under every body.
2. **The "real" hooks, same day (about 20 clips, 3 seconds each).** Change spots between them.
3. **CTAs (10 minutes).** Record all 3, plus a screen recording of someone filling out your lead form.
4. **AI hook clips:** generate them separately, using the table above.
5. **Editing:** 9:16 vertical, burned-in captions, and the hook text on screen for the first 3 seconds. Keep captions out of the bottom 20% of the frame, where Meta's buttons cover them.

**Name each ad as Body-Hook-CTA** so you can see which piece is winning: `A-H03-C1` = Body A, Hook 3, CTA 1.

**Format:** Demonstration. Gabe walking and talking on a real job, cleaning as he goes, with the crew working in the frame.
**Runtime:** about 45–60 seconds. Natural takes run longer, and that's fine.
**If your current process body is working, keep running it.** This is a second version to test next to it, and its hooks also work on your original.

**Talking points (hit all of these, in your own words):**

3. Screens get hand-washed, because a dirty screen makes a clean window look dirty. *[scrubbing screens]*

## 3. BODY B — "Trust and Reviews" (new: the trust-building body)

**Format:** Gabe talking while cleaning a window (or with the crew working behind him). Real review screenshots pop up as overlays as he mentions them. When he "reads" a review, he can pause, pull out his phone, read it, and go back to the squeegee.
**Runtime:** about 45–60 seconds.
**Built around 3 questions every homeowner silently asks before letting someone near their house: Are you insured? Are you any good? What if I'm not happy?**

**Talking points:**
1. Letting someone work on your house is a trust thing. Here are 3 questions to ask anyone.
2. **Insured?** Yes, fully. ⚠️ Background-checked crew. *[insurance certificate / tech in uniform]*
3. **What do customers say?** Read 2–3 real reviews off your phone. *[screenshot pops up for each one]* Pick ones that use the words customers use most: on time, respectful, no mess, reasonable price.
4. Almost 50 of them, all from people right around here.
5. **What if I'm not happy?** The 30-Day Spotless Guarantee.
6. That's why people keep rebooking and telling their neighbors.

**Example take:**

> Okay, so… letting somebody work on your house is, like, a trust thing, right? So, um, here's what I'd ask *anybody* before they get on a ladder at your place. Three things.
>
> First one: are you insured? We are. Fully. ⚠️ And everybody on our team's background-checked.
>
> Second, and this is the big one: what do your customers actually say? And, like, don't take my word for it. Here, *[scrolls phone]* this one's from Sarah, over in Old Trail… *[reads REAL REVIEW #1 naturally, maybe trails off partway]*
>

>
> Honestly, I could do this all day. We've got almost 50 of these, and they're all from people right around here.
>
> And then the third one: what if I'm not happy? So with us, um, if your windows aren't spotless any time in the next 30 days, we just come back and redo 'em. No charge.
>
> That's… yeah. That's kind of why people keep booking us again. And honestly, most of our new customers come from neighbors telling neighbors.

*Review placeholders: **#1** first name, neighborhood and one specific detail (e.g. "on time, took their shoes off, never seen my windows like this"). **#2** one that answers an objection (price, communication, careful with the house). **#3, optional** one about the result or rebooking.*

**Picking reviews:** use ones with a **first name, a neighborhood and a specific detail**. "Great job!" proves nothing. "Sarah in Old Trail: they showed up 5 minutes early and vacuumed my window tracks" proves a lot. Choose reviews that answer the objections you hear on the phone.

## 4. BODY C — "The Offer" (your 50%-off ad, tightened)

**Format:** Offer-driven. Gabe talking mid-job, squeegee in hand, cut with fast cleaning B-roll.
**Runtime:** about 30–40 seconds.

### The guarantee: 30-Day Spotless Guarantee

> *"If your windows aren't spotless anytime in the next 30 days, we come back and re-clean them free."*

30 days is short enough to be safe and long enough to sound confident. From November through February this will almost never get used.

**⚠️ Spring pollen:** from about March to May, pollen can coat a window in less than a week. Run this guarantee in pollen season and anyone can claim a free re-clean. For those months, either narrow it to *"If you see a streak or spot from our cleaning within 30 days, we re-clean it free"* or add "pollen not included" to the fine print. Running the full version all winter is fine.

Also: **"This week only" has to be true.** An ad can run for 3 weeks, so either reset the 8 spots each Monday or change the wording to "this month." Real scarcity is what makes it work. Fake scarcity gets noticed in a local market.

### The body

**Talking points:**
1. Half off for the first 8 people who book this week.
2. "What's the catch?" There isn't one: winter is your slow season. ⚠️ *(Or, if it's true: you're filling your route in [area].)*
3. It's the full clean (inside and out, screens, tracks, the walk-through), just half price.
4. The 30-day guarantee still applies.
5. Only 8 spots. Your windows are crying out to be cleaned, and this is the week.

**Example take:**

> So, okay, we're doing something kind of… kind of crazy this week. Half off. For the first 8 people who book.
>
> And I know what you're thinking: *what's the catch?* Honestly, there isn't one. Winter's just our slow season, and I'd rather have the crew out working than, you know, sitting around.
>
> So it's the full clean. Inside, outside, we hand-wash the screens, do the tracks, the sills, all of it. Just… half the price.
>
> Oh, and it's still got our 30-day guarantee, so if they're not spotless anytime in the next month, we come back. Free.
>
> But it's only 8 spots. Once they're gone, they're gone. So yeah, if your windows have been, like, crying out to be cleaned… this is the week.

Giving a *reason* for the discount is straight from Hopkins' *Scientific Advertising* and the *$100M Offers* scarcity chapter. A discount with no reason looks like desperation or a bait-and-switch. A discount with a reason looks like a good deal.

## 5. BODY D — "Meet the Owners" (adapted from the Mint Window Cleaning ad)

**Format:** Founder story. All three owners *working on the same house* while they talk: one on the ladder, one squeegeeing, one washing screens. The camera moves between them as each one speaks, and they jump into each other's sentences.
**Runtime:** about 45–60 seconds.

**Don't copy Mint's numbers or claims.** "1,500 reviews / highest rated in Utah" works for them because it's true. The *structure* is what you're borrowing: who we are → why we started → proof → gratitude → invitation. Your real numbers, even small ones, work in that structure. "We're a small team and the owners still do the walk-through on every house" is a selling point.

**Talking points:**
1. Who you are and the company name.
2. Why you started it (your real reason). Tell it as what *you* set out to do, never what other companies did wrong.
3. "So we built the company we'd want at our own house."
4. Real numbers: years, homes, team size, almost 50 reviews.
5. You live here, so your name's on every job.
6. Thank you, and an invitation.

## 8. Launch and testing

**Campaign setup**
- **Objective:** Leads → Instant Form, set to **"Higher intent"** (adds a review screen, so fewer junk leads). Ask for their address and which service they want (windows / power washing / both).
- **Location:** Albemarle County plus Charlottesville city. In Ads Manager use ZIP codes (22901, 22902, 22903, 22911, 22932 Crozet, 22936 Earlysville, 22940 Free Union, 22945 Ivy, 22947 Keswick, 22959 North Garden, plus the smaller rural ZIPs; type each town name in Ads Manager to confirm its code). Set it to **"People living in this location,"** not "people recently in." Otherwise you pay for UVA visitors and tourists.
- **Audience:** Broad / Advantage+. No interests. Let the hooks do the targeting.
- **Structure:** One campaign, **one ad set per body** (A, B, C, D), with that body's 10 hook ads inside. Start with about **$20–30/day per ad set**. If budget is tight, launch A (proven) and B first, then add C and D in week 2.

**Reading the results**

| Metric | What it tells you | Rule of thumb |
|---|---|---|
| **Hook rate** = 3-sec plays ÷ impressions | Whether the hook stops the scroll | 30%+ is good. Under 20% after about 1,000 impressions → kill the hook |
| **Hold rate** = ThruPlays ÷ 3-sec plays | Whether the body keeps them watching | Low across *all* hooks in an ad set → the body is the problem, not the hooks |
| **Cost per lead** | Whether the whole ad works | Your actual target. Give each ad about 2× your target CPL in spend before judging |
| **Cost per booked job** | What actually matters | Track it in a spreadsheet using the ad name (`B-H03-C1`) |

**What to do with winners**
1. **Winning hook?** Put it on the other 3 bodies (`A-H03` → `B-H03`, `C-H03`, `D-H03`). Good hooks usually work on more than one body.
2. **Winning body?** Write 10 new hooks for it next filming day. Don't change the body. Hormozi's rule: bodies wear out slowly, hooks wear out fast.
3. **Losing body?** If the hook rate is fine but the hold rate is bad on every hook, rewrite the body and keep the hooks.
4. **Every filming day:** 70% more of what won, 20% close variations of winners, 10% weird new ideas.

## 10. Voice of the customer (from Gabe's reviews and testimonials)

Use **their** words in hooks, bodies and ad copy. This is what homeowners actually notice and say. *(Source: Gabe's NYC and Cville reviews and doorstep testimonials. Use them as vocabulary only. Only quote a review on screen if it's a real TideWater Cville review.)*

**The words they use for the result:** crystal clear, spotless, sparkling, "what a difference," "windows have never looked this good," "the best they've looked since they were installed," "we can see the beautiful outdoors again," "seeing clearly for the first time in months," "loving the additional light."

**What they praise, roughly in order of how often it comes up:**
1. **Professional, polite, courteous, respectful of the house.** Shoes off inside, no mess.
2. **Reasonable price.** Mentioned constantly. Say "fair, upfront price" in bodies; let reviews say "reasonable."
3. **On time, and they do what they say they'll do (and more).**
4. **Hard-working young crew that hustles.**
5. **Thorough:** screens removed and washed, inside and out, every window, going back over spots, the walk-through at the end, a supervisor checking in.
6. **Easy to talk to, responsive, fast turnaround** ("called yesterday, here today").
7. **Handles the hard stuff:** multi-story houses, a steep back hill, switching to cleaning from the inside when needed.
8. **Neighbors refer neighbors** ("my neighbors were calling to schedule," "my picky neighbor said you did a great job, and that was all I needed to hear").

**Lines to avoid even though customers say them:** "you don't realize how dirty your windows are" and "15 years of neglect." They're fine coming from a customer about their own house, but never put them in our voice (Hopkins rule #1).

## 11. Optional Body E: the doorstep testimonial (Gabe's proven NYC format)

You already ran these in NYC. It's Hormozi's "raw testimonial" format and it's the strongest proof you can make. Film one at the end of every Cville job where the homeowner is happy and agrees to be on camera.

**Gabe asks 3 questions, every time, phone on a short tripod or held by a crew member:**
1. "How did you hear about us?" (Answers like "my neighbor recommended you" are gold for "follow the crowd.")
2. "How did we do?"
3. "Would you recommend us to your neighbors?"

**How to use it:**
- **Each testimonial is a body.** Keep it raw: ums, laughs, the house behind them. Cut in B-roll from that same job.
- **The customer's best line becomes the hook.** In NYC: *"150%."* *"I called you yesterday and you were here today."* *"My neighbor, who's very picky, said you did a great job, and that was all I needed to hear."* Put the line on screen in the first 2 seconds.
- **Rules:** real customers only, with their permission (get a quick written OK). If you give them anything for it (a discount, a free screen cleaning), disclose that in the ad. That's an FTC rule.
