## The 10 rules every idea was built on

1. **In a static ad, the photo and headline are the whole hook.** Spend 80% of your effort there. If they don't stop, nothing else matters. (`$100M Hooks`, `GOATed Ads`)
2. **Call out place + person.** "LOCAL AREA + TYPE OF PERSON" is Hormozi's all-time favorite local callout. The more local, the better: Virginians < Charlottesville < Crozet. Ogilvy found local ads do better with the town name in the headline. (`100M Leads`, Ogilvy)
3. **Show, don't tell.** Gallup studied 70 campaigns and did not find one before-and-after campaign that failed to increase sales. Proof beats claims. (Ogilvy, `Proof Checklist`)
4. **Story appeal.** The best photos make people ask, "What's going on here?" and then read to find out. (Ogilvy)
5. **Look like the buyer.** Show homeowners who look like your customers and your young, friendly crew (the "boy next door"). Keep one person or thing in focus, not crowds, and don't blow faces up bigger than life. (`100M Leads` likeness, Ogilvy)
6. **Other people saying it beats you saying it.** Raw beats polished. Newer beats older. Numbers beat no numbers. Local beats far away. (`Proof Checklist`)
7. **Specific beats vague. Simple beats clever.** Ogilvy found headlines in quote marks get 28% more recall, and "blind" or punny headlines do worse. (Ogilvy, `Headline Engine`)
8. **Spread ideas across all five awareness levels.** Curiosity for the unaware, pain for the problem-aware, promise for the solution-aware, proof for the product-aware, offer for the most aware. (`Breakthrough Advertising`, `GOATed Ads`)
9. **Clear beats clever on the CTA.** Say what to do, when, what they get, and what happens next. (`GOATed Ads`)
10. **Make lots, then multiply the winners.** Find the 2 or 3 that win, then run them through the Ad Kaleidoscope (new headline, background, border, crop, neighborhood, person) instead of starting from scratch. (`ACQ Advertising Handbook`)

**Awareness tags used below:** `U` Unaware · `P` Problem-aware · `S` Solution-aware · `Pr` Product-aware · `M` Most aware

---

## Before you run any of these

- **Use real photos, real reviews and real quotes, with the customer's OK.** Never make up a review, a text message or a "neighbor's post." It breaks FTC and Meta rules, and real proof works better anyway (raw > processed).
- **Meta won't let ads say or imply someone's age, health or similar personal traits.** "Are you over 70?" or "Widowed and living alone?" will get rejected. Use a customer quote ("I'm in my eighties, and the job is just overwhelming") or talk about the house instead.
- **Only claim what's true.** Review counts, star ratings, guarantees, plant protection, soft wash pressure, insurance: fill these in only if they're accurate for you.

## Standard CTA (use on every ad unless noted)

- **Button:** Get Quote (or Call Now)
- **Last line of the post:** "Tap **Get Quote**, enter your address, and we'll call you within [10] minutes with a price. No one has to come look first."

This covers what to do, how, when, and what happens next. A quote over the phone in minutes is also an ease benefit on its own: no stranger has to come to the house just to give a price.

### 1. The Question  `P` · proven winner

- **Image:** You (the owner) in your work shirt, holding a squeegee in front of a real local home, looking straight at the camera with a friendly half-smile. Shoot on your phone in natural light. It should look like a neighbor's photo, not a studio shot. Even better if a half-cleaned window is behind you.
- **Text on image:** "When's the last time you had your windows washed?" Small line: "[Business Name] · Charlottesville"
- **Post opener:** "Real quick question for Charlottesville homeowners: when's the last time your windows were washed?"
- **Why it wins:**
  - It has already won for you. A customer quoted it back: "Boy, you really hit me hard when you said… When's the last time you had your windows washed?" Hormozi puts 70% of every new batch on hooks that have already won.
  - Most people can't answer it, and that pause is the hook.
  - Your face is the local, trustworthy, "boy next door" signal your buyers respond to.
- **Variations:** crew photo instead of you · different house · "When were your windows last cleaned?" · add "Can't remember? That's your answer." · white text on red bar vs. black on yellow · a neighborhood name in the small line.

### 2. The Half-Clean Window  `P`

- **Image:** One outside pane cleaned on only the left half. Run a strip of painter's tape down the middle while you clean, then pull it off. Shoot straight on so the yard looks sharp through the clean half and hazy through the dirty half. Use a real customer's home, with their OK.
- **Text on image:** "One window. Half cleaned." Sub: "Which side looks like yours?"
- **Post opener:** "'You just get used to the film on them and don't realize how much light they're blocking.' A customer told us that, and this photo shows exactly what she meant."
- **Why it wins:**
  - It's a before and after in one frame, so nobody can think "that's two different windows" (raw > processed, show > tell).
  - It's Ogilvy's best-tested format, and it creates the "wow" moment in the feed instead of after the job.
  - The question makes them go look at their own windows.
- **Variations:** vertical vs. horizontal split · half-brushed screen · sliding glass door · second-story window from inside · labeled "Before / After" vs. no labels.

### 3. Low Sun  `U`

- **Image:** From inside, shoot toward a low morning or late-afternoon sun through a dirty window, so every streak and bit of film lights up. Keep a bit of the room in frame (curtain, armchair) so it's clearly a home.
- **Text on image:** "Notice this when the sun hits?" Sub: "That's not your eyes. It's years of pollen and film."
- **Post opener:** "'I'm looking at it now because the sun's streaming through, and I can see it. It just needs to be done.' Sound familiar?"
- **Why it wins:**
  - It's the exact moment your buyer first notices the problem (step 1 of the emotional journey on your avatar sheet).
  - It reaches people who aren't thinking about window cleaning at all, because curiosity is the hook for unaware people (`GOATed Ads`).
  - Next time the sun is low, they'll think of you.
- **Variations:** morning vs. evening light · kitchen vs. living room · customer quote as the headline · run heavier October to January, when the sun sits lowest.

### 4. "You remind me of my son."  `Pr`

- **Image:** You (or a young crew member) at a customer's front door, talking with the homeowner (a woman in her 60s to 80s), both smiling in a natural moment. Not a posed handshake. Eye level, normal-size faces.
- **Text on image:** "'You remind me of my son.'" Small: "A Charlottesville customer"
- **Post opener:** "A customer said this to us, and it stuck. We're a young, local crew. We show up when we say we will, take our shoes off, and put every screen back."
- **Why it wins:**
  - Headlines in quote marks get 28% more recall (Ogilvy).
  - The photo has story appeal: who are these two, and what's going on?
  - It's your #1 emotional driver: a trustworthy young local, not a risky stranger. And it's a customer saying it, not you.
- **Variations:** other real quotes: "Like the boy next door is coming over to take care of you." · "Right away, I felt like I'd be giving a neighbor business." · door vs. yard photo · new person in the photo.

### 5. The Review Screenshot  `Pr`

- **Image:** A real Google review screenshot, big and readable, with the five stars and the Google logo showing (get the reviewer's OK, or trim to a first name). Put a soft-focus after photo of that home behind it.
- **Text on image:** "[#] five-star reviews from Charlottesville homeowners." The review itself, e.g.: "Every window and screen looks fantastic and we can see the beautiful outdoors again! Plus, they were kind, polite, took their shoes off while inside."
- **Post opener:** "We didn't write this. [First name] in [Neighborhood] did."
- **Why it wins:**
  - Your buyers always check reviews.
  - Other people saying it beats you saying it, and third-party proof beats none (`Proof Checklist`).
  - Screenshot ads are one of Hormozi's five proven ad formats.
  - This one review answers three fears at once: thoroughness, screens and manners.
- **Variations:** rotate your 10 best reviews, one per ad · match the review to a fear (careful, on time, fair price, screens back in) · 4-review collage · stars-and-headline only.

### 6. The Route Ad ("Looking for 6 homes")  `M` (also works cold)

- **Image:** Mostly bold text on a solid color (the classic look), with a small photo of your truck parked on a street in that neighborhood, or in front of the neighborhood's entrance sign.
- **Text on image:** "Looking for 6 more homes in [Crozet] on [Tuesday, May 12]." Sub: "We're already in the neighborhood, so we can fit you in fast."
- **Post opener:** "We have a crew in [Crozet] on [May 12] and room for 6 more houses on the route. Window cleaning and soft washing. Once the 6 spots fill, the next [Crozet] day is [date]."
- **Why it wins:**
  - "Looking for [number] [people]" is one of the oldest direct-response ads and still works. One of Hormozi's examples is a plain static image for a local gym.
  - The route is a true reason why and true scarcity: you can only fit so many houses in a day.
  - The neighborhood name is the strongest local callout there is.
- **Variations:** one ad per neighborhood you serve (each one counts as a new ad) · change the number of spots · date in red · truck photo vs. text only.

### 7. Shoes Off  `Pr`

- **Image:** Close-up of two pairs of work boots lined up neatly on the mat just inside a front door, or a crew member slipping on shoe covers at the doorway. Bright, clean entryway.
- **Text on image:** "Yes, we take our shoes off." Small: "Local window cleaning & soft washing · [Business Name]"
- **Post opener:** "[#] of our Google reviews mention our manners. It starts at the front door."
- **Why it wins:**
  - It's a window cleaning ad that shows boots, not windows. That's story appeal, and it stops the scroll.
  - It speaks to the "strangers in my home" fear with one small, specific, believable detail.
  - It's provable: a big share of your reviews praise your manners.
- **Variations:** boots on the mat vs. shoe covers · add the review line "took their shoes off while inside" · drop cloths and towels laid out under interior windows.

### 8. It's Alive  `U` · soft wash

- **Image:** A tight close-up of green algae on vinyl siding, on the shady side of a real local house, filling the frame. Optional: one finger swipe through it showing white siding underneath.
- **Text on image:** "That green on your siding isn't dirt. It's alive."
- **Post opener:** "It's algae. It loves the shady, damp side of Virginia houses, and it keeps spreading. Rinsing it with a hose won't stop it. Our low-pressure soft wash treats it so it doesn't come right back, without blasting your siding."
- **Why it wins:**
  - It's a "secret terror under your nose" hook: something they see every day turns out to be different from what they thought (Haddad).
  - Curiosity is the strongest pull for people who don't know they have a problem.
  - It's true, so it holds up.
- **Variations:** wide shot of the green north wall · the finger-swipe stripe · green gutters · before/after of the same wall.

### 9. We Don't Pressure Wash Your House  `S` · soft wash

- **Image:** A crew member soft washing siding with a low, gentle fan of spray (not a hard jet), with plants and trim in frame and untouched.
- **Text on image:** "We don't pressure wash your house." Sub: "Low-pressure soft wash. Gentle on paint, trim and old wood."
- **Post opener:** "A lot of homeowners tell us they're scared a pressure washer will blow off paint or break old wood. Fair. That's why we soft wash."
- **Why it wins:**
  - A line that sounds backwards from an exterior cleaner makes people stop. Conflict is one of the news hooks in `100M Leads`.
  - It answers the #1 fear on your avatar sheet: rough methods damaging the house.
  - It gives a "how" (the soft wash). When everyone claims "we clean houses," the method is what makes the promise believable (*Breakthrough Advertising*, stage 3).
- **Variations:** "Your house isn't a driveway." · side-by-side of a hard jet vs. the soft fan · add a plant-protection line · "Soft wash vs. pressure wash" comparison graphic.

### 10. The House Cleaner Gap  `P`

- **Image:** A bright, tidy room with a cleaning caddy on the counter, and a window that's clearly dirty on the outside.
- **Text on image:** "Your house cleaner does the inside. We do the part they won't."
- **Post opener:** "'I have cleaning people come in every two weeks, but they do the basics. They don't do windows.' We hear this all the time."
- **Why it wins:**
  - It's an "I knew it!" hook: it confirms something they've already noticed (Haddad).
  - It calls out a big group who already pay for help, so they already believe in hiring it out.
  - It makes you an add-on to their cleaner, not a replacement.
- **Variations:** "Your cleaners do the basics. We do the outside." · split photo of caddy vs. squeegee · the customer quote as a text-only static.

### 11. WASH ME  `U`

- **Image:** "WASH ME" written with a finger in the dust and pollen on an outside pane. Shot close, so the letters and the haze are obvious.
- **Text on image** (small, bottom): "If you can write in it, it's time."
- **Post opener:** "If you can write your name in it, it's time."
- **Why it wins:**
  - Everyone knows the dirty-car joke, so it reads in half a second (simple = persuasive).
  - Humor stops the scroll, and no competitor's stock photo looks like it.
  - It works with almost no text.
- **Variations:** write your phone number in the dirt · a smiley face · "HI CROZET" (or any neighborhood) · a small handprint.

### 12. The Ladder  `P`

- **Image:** An extension ladder leaning up to a second-story window on a real house, with nobody on it. Shoot from below so the height feels real. No injury imagery.
- **Text on image:** "500,000 people are treated for ladder injuries every year." Sub: "Let us climb. (Source: CDC)"
- **Post opener:** "Your windows aren't worth a fall. We do second stories, high foyer windows and houses on hills every day, with the right gear [and full insurance]."
- **Why it wins:**
  - A specific number from a trusted source (specificity + credibility, `Headline Engine`).
  - It ties to their long-term goal: stay safe and independent in the home they love.
  - It gives a worried spouse the words to say "no more ladders."
- **Variations:** "When your spouse says, 'No more ladders.'" · "Leave the ladder to us." · stat in a red bar · ladder on a hillside lot.

### 13. The Walk-Through Promise  `S`

- **Image:** A clipboard on a porch rail with hand-checked boxes: ☑ Every window cleaned ☑ Every screen back in ☑ Every window closed and locked ☑ Walk-through with you. Or a crew member walking the house with the homeowner, pointing at a window.
- **Text on image:** "Hired someone before and had to call them back?" Sub: "We don't leave until you've walked the house with us."
- **Post opener:** "Screens left out. Windows left open. A few windows that looked skipped. If that's happened to you, here's how every one of our visits ends."
- **Why it wins:**
  - It names the "burned before" pain in your customers' own words.
  - It turns your walk-through into a named method, and named methods feel like a real thing (Haddad).
  - It lowers risk, which matters more than price to this buyer.
- **Variations:** give it a name ("The Final Walk-Through") · photo of the walk-through vs. the clipboard · the quote "some windows it looks like they missed."

### 14. Every Pin Is a Neighbor  `Pr`

- **Image:** A map of Charlottesville and Albemarle with a pin for every home you've cleaned, using real data from your CRM or Google My Maps.
- **Text on image:** "[#] homes in Albemarle County. Every pin is a neighbor."
- **Post opener:** "Odds are we've already cleaned a house on your street. Zoom in and look."
- **Why it wins:**
  - Proof with numbers, shown visually, and more proof beats less (`Proof Checklist`).
  - Closeness matters: people care most about what's near them (`100M Leads`, Ogilvy).
  - A neighbor's word closes this buyer, and this shows hundreds of neighbors at once.
- **Variations:** zoom to one neighborhood ("[23] homes in Forest Lakes") · pins in your brand color · add "Most recent: last Tuesday."

### 15. You Bought the House for This View  `S`

- **Image:** The view from inside a customer's home through a spotless window: the Blue Ridge, trees or fall color. Keep the window frame in shot so it's clearly through glass.
- **Text on image:** "You bought the house for this view." Sub: "Let's get it back."
- **Post opener:** "'Every window and screen looks fantastic and we can see the beautiful outdoors again!' That's the goal every visit."
- **Why it wins:**
  - It shows the dream outcome, not the chore. People buy the feeling, not the service.
  - It hits pride of ownership and protecting their biggest asset.
  - It speaks to your hill homes with views, which are your biggest jobs.
- **Variations:** fall color (October) · snow (January) · spring blossoms · the same view dirty vs. clean as two panels.

## Production cheat sheet for statics

- **Real photos of real local jobs, shot on your phone,** beat stock photos and graphics. Ogilvy found photos beat drawings, the `Proof Checklist` says raw beats polished, and your buyers want to see homes that look like theirs.
- **One focus per image.** One window, one person, one ladder. No crowd scenes.
- **Few words on the image:** about 10 or fewer, big and high-contrast. Frame what they're looking at with as few words as possible.
- **Headline placement:** test the top third of the image (Hormozi) against right under the photo (Ogilvy).
- **People who look like your buyers:** homeowners 45 to 85, mostly women, plus your young, friendly crew.
- **Small logo and "Charlottesville" on every ad,** so the 80% who never read the post still know who and where you are.
- **Imply motion:** mid-squeegee stroke, water drops, crew walking. Movement is on Hormozi's must-have list, even in a still.
- **Make every winner in three sizes:** 1:1 and 4:5 for the feed, 9:16 for Stories.

## How to test them

1. **Launch all 15** as separate ads to the same audience (homeowners in your service radius, 45+), with the same CTA and the same budget per ad.
2. **Judge on cost per booked job, not clicks.** Track what happens after the click (`Headline Engine`, rule 5). Hormozi's long-term-results lesson: an ad with pricier leads that book bigger jobs and refer neighbors wins.
3. **After 1 to 2 weeks, keep the top 2 or 3** and turn off the rest.
4. **Run the winners through the Kaleidoscope** (`ACQ Advertising Handbook`), 5 to 10 versions each:
   - *Remix (no new photo):* new headline, new background color, a thin border, new font, new crop/size, black-and-white or higher-contrast filter.
   - *Remake (new photo):* same idea at a new house, new neighborhood name, new person, new prop, a different season.
5. **Going forward, use 70-20-10:** 70% of new ads are versions of winners, 20% borrow winning ideas from other industries, and 10% are wild new tests from Section 9 above.
6. **Don't retire a winner because you're bored of it.** Your prospects haven't seen it yet (Henry Ford's Model T story).
